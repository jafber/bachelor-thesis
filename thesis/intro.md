- LLMs have demonstrated remarkable capabilities across a wide range of tasks.
  - Reliability limited in extended reasoning
  - Motwani 2026: GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Pro <10% accuracy on LongCoT
- Extended operation can also expose misaligned behavior in LLMs.
  - Greenblatt 2026: Hugging Face incident
- As LLM agents gain broader decision-making privileges, reliable and efficient error detection becomes essential.
  - During training and inference
- Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning makes intermediate steps available for monitoring.
  - Wei 2023: intermediate steps before final answer
  - Wang 2024: monitoring for reasoning accuracy
  - Williams 2026: monitoring for alignment
- Existing CoT monitoring approaches have four major limitations.
  - Williams 2026: expensive; OpenAI monitors only training runs flagged by cheaper probes
  - Zheng 2025: best judge achieved 71.5% on ProcessBench
  - Wang 2024: process reward models focus on verifiable math; open-ended steps harder
  - Baker 2025: models may conceal their reasoning
- This thesis investigates DC-PMI as a training-free, computationally efficient error-detection signal.
  - PMI: association between events relative to independence
  - Holtzman 2021: DC-PMI corrects common-token bias in multiple-choice answers
  - Compare answer probabilities with and without the question to isolate its contribution
  - Here: apply the same principle to intermediate reasoning steps
- This thesis makes three main contributions.
  - Qwen 3.5 error detection: >80% performance on three benchmarks
    - Zheng 2025: strong error detection; Zeng 2023 and Tyen 2024: earlier limitations
  - Low DC-PMI correlates significantly with errors in mathematical reasoning
  - DC-PMI comparable to LLM judges on tracking shuffled objects; judges generally better elsewhere
- DC-PMI captures information about step correctness, but its effectiveness varies across tasks.
  - Potential component of efficient CoT monitoring without additional training
